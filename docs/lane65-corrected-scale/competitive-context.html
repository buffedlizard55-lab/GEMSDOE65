Why the sparse family scores ~0.28 — and what beats it

The question

“Why did h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros score 0.2778, and can we beat it?”

Answer 1 — the attribution is not verified

The artifact's own page labels it UNSCORED and its manifest entry carries receipt: null. A public leaderboard is participant-level and never names an uploaded file. So the correct statement is: a file with that name was reported by its owner to score 0.2778; nothing in the records checked ties those bytes to that row. Any causal story is a mechanism hypothesis, clearly labelled as such.

Answer 2 — the mechanism is the metric's algebra, and it is verifiable here

Writing the DTI with the organizer's kernel, the marginal change from adding one unit of prediction mass at a location whose kernel credit to the nearest true fault is k satisfies (shared template metric.py, verified against a brute-force transcription of the published equations):

DTI = T / (0.2·(T + F) + 0.8·K)      F = sum over emitted mass of (1 − k)
accept a dot  ⇔  k > alpha · DTI   (alpha = 0.2)

At DTI ≈ 0.28 that bar is k > 0.056, i.e. a dot must sit within ≈ 283 m of a true fault to pay for itself, and a dense or diffuse field spreads mass at k ≈ 0, which costs 0.2 per unit of mass for nothing. That is exactly why a sparse, catalogue-hugging field beats a dense probability surface on this metric, and it is why the owner's own ladder is monotone in density: 0.26 at 44 090 emitted pixels, 0.2477 at 60 069, 0.1922 at 121 131 (all OWNER-REPORT). “Catalogue-flank B = 2 prune” is a denser-thinning step on the same family, so 0.2778 is consistent with the same mechanism — nothing exotic required.

Answer 3 — the honest gap is recall, not cleverness

The family's best owner-reported anchor is 0.2778; the public leaderboard snapshot taken 2026-10-11 (manual page fetch, rows transcribed by the family's registry) has a leader at 0.4133 and a dozen participants above 0.30. Under the algebra above, a score of 0.41 with small F implies covering roughly 36 % of the hidden truth's kernel credit, versus ≈ 23–24 % for the sparse dots family. The gap is recall of hidden faults. Sparse pruning cannot manufacture recall; only a model that generalizes off the mapped network can. That is precisely what a selection-bias-corrected PU model is for — and it is why this lane is worth another iteration despite today's tie.

Answer 4 — what we did not do

We did not claim a projection as a score, did not relabel another lane's raster, and did not treat the registered holdout as a leaderboard predictor: the family measured ρ = +0.14 between their catalogue-truth holdout and 12 live owner-reported scores, so our HOLDOUT-DTI numbers compare arms, not outcomes.
