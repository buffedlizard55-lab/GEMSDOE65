IR-65-CAT-01 — is the catalogue the known-fault compilation? (resolved)

Observed. Every topographic covariate is near-random against the catalogue (relief_local AUC 0.5633, slope 0.5577, grav_basin_fill 0.4593, lidar_cover 0.5205) but strongly predictive of the state-geologic-map-only fault pixels (relief_local 0.7740, height_above_base 0.7687, slope 0.7617). For a fault map of an extensional province that is suspicious: faults are supposed to like relief.

Measurement. scripts/diagnose_irregularities.py rasterises the mirrored QFaults/INGENIOUS trace_segments_utm11.csv (84,268 of 84,331 segments inside the grid, 1,126 lines) onto the official grid and compares:

  • catalogue pixels covered by rasterised traces: 96.54 %; catalogue-to-trace distance median 0 px, mean 0.035 px, p90 0 px, p99 1 px, max 1 px (Mann-Whitney p = 0 that catalogue pixels are closer to a trace than non-catalogue pixels);
  • all 84,331 segment rows of the mirrored file were rasterised (all_touched=True), giving 82,871 trace pixels inside the footprint, of which 71.05 % are catalogue pixels — the catalogue is a subset of the trace network, i.e. the compiled traces that were accepted;
  • morphology: 3,199 8-connected components, median 12 px, max 360 px, and only 24 px of 60,988 survive a 3×3 erosion (0.0394 %) — 1-pixel-wide lines, not areas.

Resolution. The catalogue is the USGS QFaults / INGENIOUS compilation, so its weak topographic AUCs are a real property of a geophysics-inferred inventory, not a data error. This is what makes the two-inventory differential a legitimate selection-bias signal: QFaults carries many Inferred basin-margin faults drawn from geophysics, while state geologic maps are bedrock-exposure maps. Receipt: evidence/catalogue_identity.json.

IR-65-IDENT-01 — the propensity is not identified from PU data alone (resolved)

The observed-data likelihood of a SAR PU model, L = Σ_labeled log(g·e) + Σ_unlabeled log(1 − g·e), depends only on the product g(x)e(x) = P(s=1|x). Any re-factorisation of the same product fits equally well, and the parametric class does not exclude the mirror (a logistic e mirrored about 0.5 is still logistic).

tests/test_anchor.py::test_em_direction_is_not_identified demonstrates this numerically on synthetic data with a known propensity: the EM returns both a correct-direction branch (corr > +0.5) and a mirrored branch (corr < −0.5), and their observed-data log-likelihoods differ by less than 2 %. Two earlier EM initialisations were retired for this reason (a labelled-vs-unlabelled logistic start gave Spearman −0.899; an 8-iteration constant-e0 responsibility warm start gave −0.906).

Resolution. A second public inventory anchors the direction: with q(x) = P(in catalogue | in catalogue ∪ reference, x) and reference-inventory completeness c, q = e/(e + c(1−e)) inverts to e(x;c) = c·q/(1 − q + c·q). The ordering is identified by data; c is one documented scale parameter, swept as the sensitivity analysis the brief asks for. tests/test_anchor.py::test_anchor_recovers_true_propensity_direction shows the anchor recovers the true ordering (corr > 0.6) for every c in the grid on synthetic data where the EM alone mirrors it.

IR-65-PROP-01 — the selection rule accepted a degenerate propensity (fixed)

Observed in evidence/e1_px5.json. Under the original rule ("use the EM when its Spearman against the anchor is positive"), the EM was selected in all four folds, but its propensity was near-constant: e ∈ [0.2808, 0.2961] across the whole footprint (p01–p99), mean 0.2881 on catalogue pixels vs 0.2889 off them — i.e. no usable variance and the wrong (weakly) direction. Consequences visible in the same receipt: A2_sar_1me was indistinguishable from A1_sar (paired Δ +0.000120, CI [−0.000464, +0.000825]) and every arm scored AUC ≈ 0.50 against the state-map-only instrument, where single covariates reach 0.77. A sign test on the ordering cannot detect a variance collapse.

Fix. Pre-registration amendment GEMSDOE65-PREREG-1-A1 (see registry/preregistration.json): the anchored propensity is primary and the EM becomes a reported sensitivity arm (A3_sar_em_1me). The amendment is arm-neutral — no ranking rule, risk functional, fold, seed, budget, emission geometry, gate or ship rule changed — and the pre-amendment experiment is retained and reported next to the post-amendment one. The amendment cannot make the gate pass; it only stops the mechanism from being switched off by a defect.

IR-65-NORM-01 / IR-65-SUBS-01 / IR-65-FOLD-01 / IR-65-PEEL-01 (all fixed before the gate)

  • Normaliser. Scaling the labelled SAR block by π·E_x[e] is biased when g and e correlate — and they do here. Correct normaliser is ρ = P(s=1) = n_L/(n_L+n_U) on population counts. Guarded by tests/test_risks.py::test_wrong_normaliser_pi_ebar_is_detected.
  • Subsample π. With a 20,000-row unlabeled subsample of a 5,116,066-pixel population the EM returned π = 0.867 against a truth of ≈0.02 because every subsample row was treated as one population member. Fixed with u_weight = n_population/n_subsample; guarded by both tests in tests/test_propensity_subsample.py.
  • Per-fold emission. Emitting inside each fold and OR-ing the results multiplies the scored mass by the number of folds. Replaced by out-of-fold score fields plus one global emission per arm per instrument.
  • Peel. d > peel_px admits dots at exactly 3.00 px only by accident; the champion geometry is d >= 3 px (300 m), which is what allowed_mask now enforces. Measured in the E1 receipt: min_dist_to_visible_px = 3.0.

IR-65-I2LEAK-01 — the anchor must not see the pixels it is scored on (fixed)

Instrument I2's truth is the state-map-only population, and the anchor is fitted on that same population. Fitting the anchor on all of it would make I2 an in-sample instrument and its AUC meaningless. Fix: for fold k the anchor is fitted on reference pixels outside quadrant k, and I2 is scored leave-one-quadrant-out on quadrant k. Instrument I1 needs no such treatment — its truth is withheld catalogue components, which are excluded from the anchor's catalogue sample by construction and cannot appear in the reference sample (which is >3 px from the whole catalogue).

IR-65-UNIQ-01 — the literal uniqueness rule is unmeetable for sparse fields (flagged)

The inherited rule blocks a candidate when more than 70 % of its dots fall within 3 px of any one registry raster's dots. Dense lattice comparators in the family registry (calib_r13lattice_00904.tif and friends) cover nearly the whole footprint, so the sibling repository measured 0.9985 overlap for its best candidate — a value no sparse field can avoid. This lane reports both readings side by side (the literal one over all accessible rasters, and a like-for-like one restricted to dot-field comparators, which is the only comparison in which a dot-overlap test carries information) and flags the conflict for the owner instead of waiving it. Changing the rule to get a pass would be a waiver, and this session does not make one.

Also note the inventory itself is incomplete: only sibling public-repository rasters are accessible from this sandbox, so complete_accessible_scan is false and the shared tool therefore returns unique: false regardless of the measurements.

IR-65-COV-01 — cosmetic receipt bug (open)

scripts/build_covariates.py writes mean_on_catalogue = NaN for slope and grav_basin_fill because sentinel pixels sit inside the catalogue. The lane reads nanmean over valid pixels, so no result is affected; the receipt should still be fixed upstream.

IR-65-ACCESS-01 — no outbound access (open, reported)

drivendata.org, docs.nlr.gov (rules PDF), the five Dropbox data mirrors, gdr.openei.org, usgs.gov, gbcge.org and raw.githubusercontent.com all return HTTP 000 from this sandbox. Official statements are therefore quoted from the family's line-by-line verification record (60GEMSDOE/docs/research/verification-2026-10-04.md, read 2026-10-04) with links for manual review, and are labelled as such rather than presented as freshly verified. No organizer receipt exists anywhere in this repository: nothing was uploaded and no submission slot was spent.

IR-65B-TEMPLATE-01 — two divergent pins of the shared template (contained, reported)

Observed. Merging origin/main into this branch revealed that the canonical lane-65 session vendors the shared template at vendor/template58/ (pinned to buffedlizard55-lab/58GEMSDOE tree 3ccda56f…, per-file hashes in docs/data/shared_template_pin.json), while this session had committed its own copy of the same template under src/gems57/. Because gems57 is a single module name per Python process, the copy imported first wins for everything imported afterwards: with src/ on sys.path, this session's copy shadowed the canonical pin and tests/test_submission.py::test_writer_actual_tiff_and_single_member_zip_roundtrip failed (it asserts NaN outside the footprint; the shadowing writer produced zeros).

Contained. src/gems57/ was moved to vendor/template60/ with a provenance file (vendor/template60/PROVENANCE.md) that records the measured SHA-256 divergence per module: __init__, emit, evaluate_holdout, metric, network, spatial are byte-identical between the pins; gates, grid, holdout, submission_writer, uniqueness, validate differ. scripts/_bootstrap.py puts vendor/template60 first for this lane's scripts only and then asserts which copy actually loaded (assert_template60), so a silent version swap cannot change a published number — the guard caught a real mis-wiring while it was being added. Test modules reach gems57 through the canonical pin (from gems65 import common), so the shared pytest process loads exactly one template. Full suite after the change: 113 passed, and scripts/check_repository.py reports ok: true with no problems.

What this means for the numbers. Every HOLDOUT-DTI on this lane's pages comes from evaluate_holdout.py and metric.py, which are byte-identical to the canonical pin, and the fold quadrant assignment (holdout.quadrant_ids) is byte-identical too — so the DTI arithmetic is reproducible under the canonical template. The fold constructor is not: template58 has no buffered_component_draw (it exposes build_buffered_holdout_context / HoldoutContext and marks build_holdout legacy). Porting this lane's instruments to that API would change the folds and therefore every number, so it is left as the owner's consolidation step rather than being done silently at merge time.

Not a private fork. Nothing in vendor/template60/ was edited; it is a labelled second pin, reported here, per the standing rule "if a shared tool is wrong, fix it once in the template and report it; never keep a private fork".

IR-65B-FORMAT-01 — the two pins contradict each other on the portal format (flagged)

The canonical pin (vendor/template58/gems57/grid.py) says mode="nan" "is the submission representation" and that mode="zeros" "is not portal-format compliant". This lane's pin (vendor/template60/gems57/grid.py) says the opposite: mode="zeros" "is the only submission-compatible mode" and mode="nan" is "for local diagnostics only … a prior portal range error does not establish NaN as its cause".

This artefact was written in all-finite zeros mode, because (a) the organizer's own reference solution (https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution) writes an all-finite raster with no nodata tag, (b) the official format statement recorded in the family's verification notes says values must be in [0, 1] with null/NaN outside the bounds — which zeros also satisfies, since 0 is inside [0, 1] and the scored footprint is what the metric reads, and (c) the portal rejection this lane was asked to fix ("Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]") arrived with NaN primaries. The contradiction cannot be settled from this sandbox (drivendata.org is unreachable, IR-65-ACCESS-01), so it is flagged: if the owner prefers the canonical NaN representation, scripts/make_submission.py should write both variants — the sibling session already publishes -nan.tif and -zeros.tif pairs, which is the safest convention and is recommended here.

IR-65B-PARALLEL-01 — two sessions ran the same lane (resolved by integration)

The parallel-run protocol anticipates this. Session arena/1217c9a0-gemsdoe65 implemented the same SAR-PU lane first and merged PRs #7 and #9 (canonical site, RUN_CARD.json, verdict NEGATIVE, six published rasters behind a loud not-cleared banner). This session (arena/98cb5eb2-gemsdoe65) implemented it independently: different covariate pipeline (covariates_sar2inv.py), different propensity estimator (a two-inventory anchor with the EM as a sensitivity arm, versus an EM with a Horvitz-Thompson π), different instruments (leave-one-quadrant-out I2), different emission geometry (3 px NMS with d >= 3 peel at a 38,000-dot budget), and a pre-registered ship rule with an amendment trail.

Both sessions reached a negative verdict, for different reasons. That agreement is the useful result: two independent implementations of the same lane, with different propensity estimators and different instruments, both fail their own pre-registered gates. Nothing was overwritten in the merge — the canonical site, run card, guards and tests are untouched, and this session's pages live under docs/lane65b/ with its own artefact, run card and evidence files. Drift check between the two lanes' artefacts is reported below.

Cross-session drift check (the protocol's duplicate test, applied to the two lanes' published rasters rather than to the registry at large; receipt evidence/cross_session_drift.json, script scripts/cross_session_drift.py): against all 6 rasters the sibling session published, worst Spearman ρ = 0.0170 (limit 0.9), worst forward dot overlap = 0.2873 (limit 0.7), worst Jaccard = 0.0122 (limit 0.5) → duplicate_by_any_limit = false. The informative number is the overlap: 28.7 % of this lane's dots land within 300 m of the sibling lane's best-matching field — far above chance for two sparse 38,000-dot fields on 5.17 M pixels, far below the duplicate threshold. Two independent implementations of the same lane agree on roughly a quarter of placements and disagree on the rest, which is consistent with both verdicts being negative for different reasons.
