0. Why this verdict — and the one decision only the owner can make

The pre-registered gate is keyed to instrument I1, and the lane fails it. Paired Δ(A1_sar − B_nnpu) on I1 = -0.014874, 95% CI [-0.023885, -0.005936] — the CI excludes zero in the losing direction, so this is not a coin flip. The secondary tercile condition also fails (Δ -0.014073, CI [-0.036482, +0.003093]). The ship rule therefore returns DO-NOT-SUBMIT and this session does not override it.

On the instrument that matches the live task, the same lane wins clearly. I2's truth is mapped faults the catalogue does not contain (59,012 px): A2_sar_1me 0.140842 [0.132499, 0.148127] vs nnPU 0.101953 [0.091793, 0.113242] and vs the proximity control 0.021906 [0.017568, 0.027346]. Paired Δ(A1_sar − B_nnpu) on I2 = +0.024246 [+0.014802, +0.032843] and Δ(A1_sar − A2_sar_1me) = -0.014643 [-0.022646, -0.007147], both CIs excluding zero in the winning direction. Pooled ranking AUC against I2 truth: A2 0.7145 > A1 0.6311 > B 0.5098 > A3(EM) 0.4976 > proximity 0.4806.

The two instruments have opposite sign on the same question, and the reason is structural, not statistical. I1's withheld truth is made of catalogue components contiguous with visible catalogue trace, and the organizer masks known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels out of scoring (staff answer, community thread 11516) — so I1 rewards exactly the pixels that cannot score live. Consistently, the trivial proximity control is the best arm on I1 (0.090711) and the worst on I2 (0.021906). The family's own ladder points the same way: deleting dots 1–2 px from the catalogue raised the live score twice (+0.0108, +0.0070, owner-report).

Decision required from the owner (not made here): re-key the primary gate from I1 to I2 —or better, to a new I3 that holds out whole state-map segments with the catalogue fully masked — and re-run the ship rule. That is an amendment to a pre-registration after results are known, so it is an owner decision, not an agent one. Until then the artefact below is research-only: downloadable, fully validated, uniquely ranked (worst Spearman ρ vs 62 prior rasters = 0.0140), and marked DO-NOT-SUBMIT.

1. What was built

Lane: selection-bias-aware PU learning under SAR (selected at random given covariates), not SCAR. The competition catalogue is treated as positives selected with a covariate-dependent probability e(x) = P(labeled | fault, x). The model is trained with the propensity-aware empirical risk of Bekker, Robberechts & Davis (arXiv:1809.03207), weights w = min(1/e, 10) and v = min((1−e)/e, 10), against a non-negative PU (nnPU, Kiryo et al. 2017) baseline on identical folds, identical architecture, identical seed and an identical 38,000-dot budget. The shipped ranking multiplies the class posterior by (1 − e(x)), which pushes mass into environments the catalogue under-samples.

The identifiability problem, and how this run handles it

The observed-data likelihood of a SAR PU model is L = Σlabeled log(g·e) + Σunlabeled log(1 − g·e): it depends only on the product g(x)e(x) = P(s=1|x). The propensity is therefore not identified from PU data alone — the brief anticipates exactly this. tests/test_anchor.py::test_em_direction_is_not_identified demonstrates it numerically: the Bekker & Davis EM returns both a correct-direction and a mirrored solution on synthetic data with a known propensity, and their observed-data log-likelihoods differ by less than 2 %. This run resolves it two ways, both pre-registered:

  1. A second public inventory anchors the direction. The catalogue is compared with the mirrored state-geologic-map fault inventory (82,151 state-map pixels inside the footprint, of which only 3,718 are in the catalogue). Fitting q(x) = P(in catalogue | in catalogue ∪ state-map-only, x) and inverting q = e/(e + c(1−e)) gives e(x;c) = c·q/(1 − q + c·q): the ordering is identified by data and one documented parameter c (reference-inventory completeness) sets the scale. c = 1.0 was used; the c-grid [0.25, 0.5, 1.0] is the sensitivity analysis the brief asks for.
  2. The EM is still run (but rejected for direction). Spearman(EM ordering, anchor ordering) on the pooled two-inventory sample = 0.5983; the pre-registered rule is "use the EM only if that is > 0". Chosen source: anchor. anchor primary per the brief (a public reference index exists); the EM is reported as a sensitivity arm. EM diagnostics: Spearman vs anchor +0.598, std(e) 0.00140 vs anchor std(e) 0.25147

Measured propensity: mean e on catalogue pixels 0.6329 vs 0.3789 on independent-inventory off-catalogue fault pixels — the direction the hypothesis requires. Class prior π = 0.02322 (MEASURED: the union of both public inventories is a lower bound on fault-pixel prevalence); ρ = P(s=1) is computed from population counts, never from the unlabeled subsample (tests/test_propensity_subsample.py pins the bug this fixes: an unweighted subsample gave π = 0.867 against a truth of ≈0.03).

8. Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778, and what beats it

Full derivation: research/why-02778.html (owner-reported score; no organizer receipt ties it to exact bytes). In brief: with the dot algebra DTI = T / (0.2(T+S−M) + 0.8|G|) that raster puts 37,654 dots 0 px off the catalogue, keeps a minimum distance of 3.00 px, lands only 5.77 % of dots within 300 m of the hidden truth, and earns a mean credit of 0.135 per dot ≈ 2.4× the break-even 0.0556. Mass-pruning is nearly spent (+0.003/+0.004 per rung). The only large remaining prize is re-ranking the same mass: raising credit/dot to 0.150 ≈ 0.30, to 0.19 ≈ 0.36. A propensity-aware ranker is aimed exactly at that prize, because credit per dot is a statement about where unmapped faults are, and (1 − e(x)) is a statement about where the catalogue is blind.

9. Limitations, and what access would remove them

  • No organizer confirmation of anything. This sandbox cannot reach drivendata.org, docs.nlr.gov (the rules PDF), the five Dropbox data mirrors, gdr.openei.org, usgs.gov or raw.githubusercontent.com (all HTTP 000). Every official statement on this site is quoted from the family's line-by-line verification record (60GEMSDOE/docs/research/verification-2026-10-04.md) with links for manual review. Access needed: a machine with outbound HTTPS to those hosts, or a mirrored rules PDF.
  • HOLDOUT-DTI is not the live metric's target. I1 rewards proximity to visible traces (the withheld truth is contiguous with visible trace); the live board does not — the family's ladder measured that deleting dots 1–2 px from the catalogue raised the live score twice (+0.0108, +0.0070, owner-reported). Both instruments are reported; neither is a projection.
  • The anchor assumes the reference inventory's propensity is constant in x. State geologic maps are bedrock-exposure maps, so if they under-sample buried basin faults the anchor over-corrects toward exposed ground. This is the named non-fault process that could mimic the hypothesis (compiler-vintage and compiler-rule confounding).
  • The range front is a proxy (50 m isoline of height above a 5 km local minimum), not a mapped front; a real front needs raw 3DEP 10 m/1 m tiles, unreachable here.
  • LiDAR layer provenance: quantised scarp descriptors derived in a sibling repo from public 3DEP tiles — 10 failed tiles of 716, a 1–2 m datum offset, and they are terrain descriptors, not fault detections (roads, channels, terraces, shorelines, landslides and mines also make steps). Flagged PUBLIC-REPOSITORY-DERIVATIVE, not organizer-provided.
  • Uniqueness inventory is incomplete: 62 accessible rasters from sibling public repositories, not the organizer's registry. complete_accessible_scan = False.
