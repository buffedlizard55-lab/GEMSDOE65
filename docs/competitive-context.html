What the cited 0.2778 does—and does not—mean

The named H33 TIFF cannot be treated as the source of a 0.2778 score. The public leaderboard and the project page are separate evidence sources, and no file-level receipt links them.

Correction to the premise

The GEMSDOE32 page labels its named H33 artifact “UNSCORED” and explicitly says that no organizer score exists for artifacts in that repository. The official public leaderboard snapshot read on 2026-10-11 UTC records the PUBLIC-LEADERBOARD-OBSERVATION value 0.2778 for participant extradr19 at public rank 23; it does not expose the participant's submitted filename. There is no evidence that the H33 file generated that leaderboard row.

Why the score cannot be explained from the H33 download

The official leaderboard reports participant-level best public DTI, not artifact filenames, raster hashes, predicted-pixel counts, or model details. The GEMSDOE32 project page describes H33 as a candidate and says it is unscored. Therefore the available sources do not show that the score 0.2778 came from that raster, and they do not show why any particular model earned that public score. An explanation claiming otherwise would be invented.

What can be said from the official metric definition is that GEMS rewards predictions near expert-labelled faults with a triangular distance weight and sets the false-negative penalty higher than the false-positive penalty. Thus, in principle, a useful high-recall prediction field that puts confidence near withheld fault traces can outperform an overly sparse field; broad off-target confidence still incurs false-positive cost. This is a property of the metric—not evidence about the participant's method or the H33 TIF.

Can GEMSDOE65 exceed the cited value?

Possible in principle; not established here. This checkout has no experiment data, required shared hide-and-recover evaluator, submission writer, or registry; it has no HOLDOUT-DTI or CI and no new TIFF. The only defensible verdict is negative until the SAR candidate beats the current holdout best and matched nnPU baseline overall, passes the highest-propensity-tercile gate and the final raster passes uniqueness and format checks. A projection, a past site's score, or an unscored H33 candidate is not sufficient to answer “yes.”

The project lane's useful scientific angle is not to tune a past dot pattern. It is to model the probability that a fault gets catalogued, using a small, predeclared observability set and defensible uncertainty analysis. This can correct a known limitation in the training labels, but the hidden-fault objective makes independent validation and conservative submission selection essential.

Do not upload a TIF based on the score comparison on this page. The status page has the authoritative project gate; it currently says no approved file.
