Why the lane still matters

The competition metric rewards recovering hidden faults inside 300 m while punishing emitted mass that sits far from any fault. The family's own owner-reported live anchors show a sparse catalogue-following field scoring 0.26–0.2778 and its denser variants scoring 0.19–0.25, i.e. the sparse strategy is already stretched. The remaining headroom is recall of faults that the published compilations never mapped — exactly the quantity a selection-bias-corrected model is supposed to improve. This session tested that idea honestly and got a tie — and then found that the propensity fit had been silently degenerate because of a unit bug, fixed it, re-ran the whole comparison with an active correction, and still got a tie (+0.14 %, CI straddling zero, on the only instrument that contains the bias). What the numbers say next is a placement problem, not a propensity problem: this candidate's dots sit a median of 12.5 px from the catalogue and number 28 047, more mass than the estimated hidden truth, which the metric's own algebra cannot reward. The cheapest large lever is therefore the emission geometry — see Hypotheses #1.
