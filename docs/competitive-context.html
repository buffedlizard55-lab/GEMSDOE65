What can be established about the cited score, and what it implies

Two questions are answered separately here: (1) is the 0.2778 figure a verified score for that artifact? and (2) if it is real, what does a value near 0.28 mean geologically, and can it be beaten? Mixing the two would be speculation dressed as evidence.

Correction to the premise — the figure is not receipt-backed

The GEMSDOE32 page for its named H33 artifact labels it “UNSCORED”. Its submission manifest carries no receipt for that file, and the related PR #9 describes an owner-reported development value, not an organizer-confirmed score. A leaderboard row is participant-level and does not identify a filename, a raster hash, or a run. So the strict answer to “why is that file at 0.2778?” is: unknown — the file-to-score premise is not verified. What follows is the mechanistic reading of the number as a number, valid for any distance-weighted Tversky emission in that band.

1 · Decoding the artifact name without pretending more than it says

h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros parses as: lane hypothesis h33, family/index 2, batch b2, an explicit UTC build stamp (2026-10-04T22:00Z), an 8-hex content digest prefix, and the zeros footprint encoding (finite zeros outside the study footprint rather than NaN). It is a dot-lattice family artifact: “2” is consistent with a 2 px (200 m) lattice step, the natural choice for a 300 m kernel, because a 2 px lattice guarantees every cell inside its corridor at least 1 − 2·√2/300 ≈ 0.53 of full kernel credit. Formatting therefore cannot explain a 0.2778; placement and mass budget must.

2 · What 0.2778 implies about the emission

With α = 0.2 (false positives), β = 0.8 (false negatives) and the max-over-kernel rule, a score of 0.28 corresponds to roughly one third of the scored truth volume recovered within 300 m while spending a false-dot mass of the same order as the truth mass. In the regime table on the reasoning page that is squarely trace-level with gaps: the corridors are drawn in the right structural places (basin margins, range fronts, the magnetic and gravity lineament fabric), the traces inside them are partly offset, partly truncated, and partly absorbed by non-fault lineaments — dune crests, pediment edges, cut-banks, shorelines, roads and canals, which all look linear in a 100 m stack.

Geologically, three ceilings explain why the family’s artifacts cluster in the 0.15–0.30 range rather than higher: (i) resolution — 0.5–5 m scarplets against a 100 m grid, so half-cell mislocation already costs a third of the kernel credit; (ii) target population — the private labels are the step-overs and splays that were missed, which by construction sit where the 100 m stack is ambiguous, at a measured stand-off of 0.4–2 km from the mapped traces; (iii) mass budget — β/α = 4 rewards coverage, so a corridor sweep spends thousands of dots whose individual false-positive cost is small but whose aggregate denominator share is large.

3 · Is a submission above 0.3195 / 0.3774 achievable?

On the official record: yes for other teams. The official leaderboard (linked, never mirrored or machine-read here) carried values above both cited figures; the strongest value observed during this project’s last authorized manual audit of the board was 0.4133. Two structural features of the competition also favour well-built submissions: the initial round scores against the fixed private new-fault set while the final round rescores the same submissions against an expert-expanded label set, which rewards recall-seeking corridor emissions over thin precision picks; and the metric’s β = 0.8 makes a missed trace four times as expensive as a stray dot.

For this lane specifically: reported, not projected. The repository ships a unique candidate whose behaviour is measured on a deliberately harder instrument — whole fault components withheld with their neighbourhood context removed — and every such number is labelled HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator version, withheld-positive count and 95 % CI. This project does not convert that measurement (or any fold value) into an expected leaderboard score; a projection is never written as a score here. Whether this particular raster beats 0.3195 is an empirical question for the organizer’s private set, and the honest answer is: unknown, with the measured hold-out evidence and the uniqueness certificate stated instead.
