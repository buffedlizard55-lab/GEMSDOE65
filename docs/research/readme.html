Tectonic-geomorphology lane: channel steepness and knickpoints, not scarp shape. Kirby and Whipple (2012) review how bedrock-channel steepness and knickpoints record spatial gradients in rock uplift, including across active normal faults. Perron and Royden's χ transform (ESPL, 2013) puts different channels on a comparable footing, and Gailleton et al. (Earth Surface Dynamics) give a reproducible knickpoint-segmentation method. Scarps degrade and bury, but channel profiles keep recording base-level steps, so this can see faults the curvature features no longer show. Extract drainage from the free 3DEP 10 m DEM (or 1 m tiles), compute χ and normalized steepness, and keep only knickpoints and steepness steps that align across several independent drainages along a common strike. First test: do channels crossing withheld segments show larger steepness steps than random lines of the same orientation? If not, stop. Map and mask the usual confounds: lithology, climate gradients, ephemeral channels and playa base levels. Output the standard validated GeoTIFF, uniqueness-checked against every earlier raster.

Limitations and the access that would remove them

  • No outbound access to the organizer or to USGS. From this sandbox drivendata.org, docs.nlr.gov (rules PDF), the five Dropbox data mirrors, gdr.openei.org, usgs.gov, gbcge.org and raw.githubusercontent.com all return HTTP 000. Official statements are quoted from the family's line-by-line verification record (60GEMSDOE/docs/research/verification-2026-10-04.md) with links for manual review. Needed: a host with outbound HTTPS to those domains, or a mirrored rules PDF.
  • The holdout may not represent the live target. I1 rewards proximity to visible traces (withheld truth is contiguous with visible trace); the live board does not — the family measured that deleting dots 1–2 px from the catalogue raised the live score twice (+0.0108, +0.0070, owner-report). Both instruments are reported; neither is a projection.
  • The propensity anchor assumes the reference inventory is unbiased in x. State geologic maps are bedrock-exposure maps; if they under-sample buried basin faults, the anchor over-corrects toward exposed ground. This is the named mimicking process (inventory vintage and compiler-rule confounding), and it is the largest single risk in the lane.
  • The range front is a proxy (50 m isoline of height above a 5 km local minimum), not a mapped front. A real front needs raw 3DEP 10 m/1 m tiles — unreachable here.
  • LiDAR layer provenance: quantised scarp descriptors derived in a sibling repo from public 3DEP tiles — 10 failed tiles of 716, a 1–2 m datum offset, and they are terrain descriptors, not fault detections. Tagged PUBLIC-REPOSITORY-DERIVATIVE, not organizer-provided.
  • Uniqueness inventory is incomplete. Only sibling public-repository rasters are accessible, and the literal rule (≤70 % of dots within 3 px of any registry raster) is unmeetable for sparse fields because dense lattice comparators cover nearly the whole footprint (the family measured 0.9985). Both the literal and the like-for-like readings are reported; the rule is flagged for the owner, never silently waived.

Three-pass review

  1. Implement/verify — covariate build, risk functionals with Monte-Carlo and analytic gradient checks, EM, anchor, folds, emission, artefact writer, site.
  2. Review/fix — four real bugs found and fixed in this lane's own code and one in the inherited usage pattern: (i) the SAR normaliser must use ρ = P(s=1) on population counts, not π·E[e] (biased when g and e correlate — guarded by test_wrong_normaliser_pi_ebar_is_detected); (ii) an unweighted unlabeled subsample inflated π to 0.867 against a truth of ≈0.03 (guarded by tests/test_propensity_subsample.py); (iii) two EM initialisations converge to the mirrored factorisation — both retired and replaced by the SCAR-identity start plus the external anchor; (iv) per-fold emissions OR-ed across folds multiplied scored mass by n_folds — replaced by out-of-fold score fields and one global emission per arm; (v) the peel used d > peel_px where the champion geometry is d >= 3 px — corrected.
  3. Re-check against the request — see docs/research/review-pass3.md.

Core values applied

Maximize P(Win): the scarce resource is a weekly submission slot, so nothing is promoted without a pre-registered gate, a control arm, and a leakage canary; the lane deliberately spends zero slots and reports a verdict instead of a hope. Own the Outcome: negative results, non-identifiability proofs, retired initialisations and an unmeetable uniqueness rule are all published as deliverables rather than hidden.

Suggestions for the next session (ranked by expected value per hour)

  1. Fix the instrument before fixing the model. I1 (hide-and-recover) rewards dots that hug visible traces, because withheld truth is contiguous with visible trace; the live board does the opposite (the family measured that deleting dots 1–2 px from the catalogue raised the live score twice, +0.0108 and +0.0070, owner-report). Build an I3: hold out whole state-map segments with the catalogue fully masked, so the truth population is "mapped faults the catalogue does not contain" everywhere, not just beyond 300 m. Until the instrument agrees in sign with the live target, no gate result can be trusted in either direction.
  2. A stronger ranker. The five pinned covariates give pooled AUC 0.667 on I1 and ≈0.50 on I2; the official 19-band stack is right there and unused by this lane. Gradient boosting over all 19 bands (with the propensity still anchored on two inventories) is the cheapest large gain available, and it is inside the lane: the lane constrains the risk functional and the selection model, not the feature set.
  3. H65-4 en-echelon stepover / relay-ramp gaps (docs/research/hypotheses-2026-10-11.md): the catalogue is 3,199 fragmented 1-px traces with median 12 px, so unmapped linkage geometry between segment tips is abundant and no sibling lane has scored it. Narrow (a few thousand candidate pixels) but it can be combined with the propensity ranking instead of replacing it.
  4. H65-2 LiDAR-conditional emission. LiDAR covers 75.37 % of the footprint; the final-round truth is an expert panel's map. Spend budget preferentially where the panel will have 1 m LiDAR, and require two independent lines of evidence where it does not.
  5. Get one organizer receipt. Everything in this repo is HOLDOUT-DTI or MEASURED. One cleared submission, with its score, timestamp, file sha256 and remaining slot count copied into evidence/, converts the family's ladder arithmetic (credit/dot 0.135 → 0.150 ≈ 0.30) from owner-report into evidence. That is the selector step, not this lane.
  6. Full re-train sensitivity over π and c. This lane swept the reference-inventory completeness c at the ranking level and measured π from the two-inventory union (0.02322, a lower bound); re-training per (π, c) pair is 8 more fits and would show whether the SAR risk or only the ranking carries the effect.
  7. Resolve IR-65-UNIQ-01 with the owner. The literal 70 %-within-3-px rule cannot be met by any sparse field while dense lattice comparators are in the registry (the family measured 0.9985). That needs a written amendment to the protocol, not a per-session waiver.
  8. Fix IR-65-COV-01 upstream (mean_on_catalogue = NaN for two covariates in the build receipt) and push the gems65 normaliser/subsample fixes into the shared template if any sibling lane adopts SAR — the standing instruction is to fix shared tools once, in the template, and report it.
  9. Port the anchor. The two-inventory propensity anchor is not specific to faults: any PU competition with two overlapping public inventories of the same objects can identify the selection direction this way. It is worth extracting into the shared template with its tests.
