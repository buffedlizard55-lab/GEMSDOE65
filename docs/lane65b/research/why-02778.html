What GEMSDOE65 adds to this answer

The analysis below establishes, from committed bytes and the owner's score ledger, that h33-h33-2-b2 (0.2778, [owner-report] — no organizer receipt ties the score to exact bytes) is the last rung of a mass-pruning ladder, that its dots earn a mean credit of 0.135 per dot ≈ 2.4x the break-even 0.0556, and that pruning is nearly exhausted (+0.003/+0.004 per rung). Its own conclusion is that the only large remaining prize is re-ranking the same mass (credit/dot 0.135 -> 0.150 ~= 0.30, -> 0.19 ~= 0.36).

This lane attacks exactly that prize, and it does so with a selection model rather than a new positive-evidence layer:

  1. Credit per dot is a statement about where unmapped faults are. The competition masks known USGS/INGENIOUS faults out of scoring, so every scorable dot must land on a fault the catalogue does not contain. Ranking by P(fault | x) alone therefore spends budget on ground where the catalogue is already dense.
  2. Where the catalogue is blind is measurable, not guessed. Two independent public inventories of the same ground disagree systematically (this repo, evidence/mapping_bias_two_inventory.json, [measured-here]): relative to a state geologic map fault layer, the catalogue over-represents deep-basin-fill, low-relief, low-slope ground far from the range front (bias AUCs 0.20-0.34) and under-represents exposed high-relief steep ground (0.74-0.80 inverted). 1 - e(x) is precisely that under-representation, estimated per pixel.
  3. The propensity is not identified from PU data alone, so it is anchored externally. tests/test_anchor.py demonstrates the mirror factorisation numerically; the anchor inverts q = e/(e + c(1-e)) from the two-inventory comparison and leaves one documented scale parameter c. This is the brief's "report it as a sensitivity analysis" branch, with data doing the direction-setting.
  4. Consequence for the ladder: the same 38,000-dot budget, re-ranked by P(y=1|x)*(1-e(x)), is the intervention the 0.2778 analysis says is worth ~0.02-0.08 on the live board if the two-inventory differential tracks the expert panel's map. That conditional is the whole risk of this lane, and it is why both instruments (I1 hide-and-recover, I2 state-map-only) are reported instead of one.

1b. The geometry that the ladder's numbers actually imply

[measured-here] the champion's 37,654 dots are not a ribbon on the mapped traces. Of its dots, 0 lie on the catalogue, only 5.77 % lie within 300 m of it, and the median dot sits 1.97 km away (p95 = 8.19 km, max 21.9 km). The champion is therefore a wide-field emission whose mass is mostly 1–20 km from the published network, and the two most recent rungs removed the small part of it that did lie in the catalogue's immediate 100–200 m halo. The field underneath it (H19-5 → d1.5 → d2.8) was built from convergent multi-layer evidence; thinning kept the ranking while cutting the mass, and peeling cut the masses that sat closest to the published map.

1c. Why the hidden faults are "off-catalogue" at all — the physical case

The prize's truth is a set of new expert-mapped faults, so by construction it excludes the existing USGS/INGENIOUS compilation. Four physical mechanisms put real, mappable fault trace off that compilation while keeping it near real structure:

  1. Compilation gaps, not geological gaps. The published network is drawn from state maps of differing vintage and resolution; mapping stops at county, range and land-status boundaries and where young alluvium hides the trace. The fault continues; the polygon does not.
  2. Distributed and secondary deformation. Trenching and LiDAR campaigns repeatedly show displacement on splays, step-overs and antithetic strands hundreds of metres off the principal trace — real trace that a regional compilation does not carry.
  3. Low-slip-rate and short faults. Structures below the compilation's inclusion threshold (short, poorly expressed, no dated displacement) are exactly the population a "new faults" panel can add.
  4. Detection-technology change. The panel uses data the compilation never saw (bare-earth LiDAR, modern geophysics), so it finds faults that were always there and never mapped.

The champion's ladder is the empirical fingerprint of (1)–(4): the hidden set is close enough to the published network that a wide-field emission scores, and the published trace itself is not the answer — mass on the published halo was worth less than nothing (its removal paid twice).

2. The algebra that governs every decision here

[official] DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w), triangular kernel k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0), α = 0.2, β = 0.8, R = 300 m (page 967), with the official worked example TP_w = 3.00, FP_w = 1.89, FN_w = 2.00 → 0.60 reproduced exactly by src/gems57/metric.py [measured-here] (brute-force test in tests/test_metric.py, agreement 1e-9).

With T = TP_w, S = Σp (emitted mass), k_x the self-credit of a dot (its own best kernel weight towards any truth pixel) and M = Σ_x k_x:

DTI = T / ( 0.2·(T + S − M) + 0.8·|G| )                     (★)
add one dot with marginal coverage dT and self-credit k:
   dDTI > 0   ⟺   dT > 0.2·DTI·(dT + 1 − k)                (★★)
non-redundant dot (dT = k):  pays iff  k > 0.2·DTI

At the champion's 0.2778 the bar is k > 0.0556: a dot pays if it is within 283 m of an uncovered hidden-truth pixel. At the leader's 0.4133 the bar is 0.0827.

What (★) says about the champion. Its dots lie 0.3–21.9 km from the catalogue, so their self-credit is driven by the hidden set alone. Under the repo's own calibration of that set (src/gems32/live_anchor.py, |G| ≈ 12,226 px [model] derived from the owner-reported ladder), the champion's implied credit is T ≈ 0.2778·(0.2·(37,654 − M) + 0.8·12,226 + 0.2T); with M = T (thin-dot regime) this is T ≈ 5,092 px of weighted recall, i.e. ≈ 42 % of the hidden mass, with a mean credit of 0.135 per dot — 2.4× the break-even bar. Those are the two numbers to beat. Three independent readings of the same ladder (0.1922 → 0.2778, the LM live-mirror, and the knowledge/01 inversion) agree to within a few percent, so the scale is trustworthy even though |G| itself is unverified.

3. Can we beat 0.2778? Yes — but only in one of three ways, and two of them are nearly spent

(a) Remove worthless mass — half spent. Every removal so far paid at a nearly constant rate: 2.78e-6 and 2.75e-6 score per removed dot [measured-here from [owner-report] scores]. A third shell (1,091 dots at d ≈ 3 px) and a fourth (1,487 at d ≈ 4 px) are still in the file [measured-here]. If the rate held, those two shells are worth ≈ +0.003 and +0.004; it will not hold indefinitely, because the organizer's masking halo is finite — but how finite is exactly what a peel ladder measures, at one upload per rung.

(b) Re-rank the same mass — the field-quality route, and the only big prize. (★) is a ratio: raising the mean credit per dot from 0.135 to 0.150 lifts the score to ≈ 0.30; to 0.19 lifts it to ≈ 0.36. The public board shows this is achievable: **#1 xiaofanhu 0.4133, #2 JerryDataWorks 0.3418,

3 alexoktaba 0.3361 [official, snapshot 2026-10-11]**, i.e. 23 % above the champion sits a

score that is not reachable by mass control alone. Nothing in this repository has held a population-matched instrument edge over the champion until this session (§4).

(c) Identification, not guessing — two uploads, already built. h69A (0.5 × champion) and h69B (champion + 2,000 floor dots) identify T, K = |G| and F exactly from two live returns plus the already-paid anchor [measured-here: files built, scripts/h69_recovery.py self-test exact]. Neither probe can lower the standing (both are constructed below the anchor by the (★) arithmetic with every plausible parameter set).

5. Ranked hypotheses (expected DTI gain × cost, with the reason for the order)

Read this first: under the governing lane STOP and the literal uniqueness rule none of these items is submittable today (§4c) — the ranking is the order in which they produce evidence, and the first owner decision is the governance one, not the experiment one.

The empirical fact that drives the order. The champion's own field is good enough; what is missing is (i) knowledge of the hidden set's size and of how much credit each dot really earns locally, and (ii) mass placed where the incumbent's kernels do not reach. The five items below are ordered by evidence already in hand per slot spent.

1. H72 — union: champion ∪ convergent-cover complement (built, measured; NOT CLEARED to submit). Layers: the champion's own multi-layer field (topo gap closure / Δksn family) plus the H71 convergent-evidence field (3DEP-10 m Δksn, ksn, knickpoint reduction; 10 m relief, profile curvature, hillshade lineament; distance to state-map faults >300 m off the catalogue). Physical signature: a complementary population of real fault trace 0.4–6 km from the mapped network, where several independent layers co-fire. Mimic named: drainage/erosion lineaments and playa-margin straight edges — suppressed by the ≥3-layer co-fire rule and the

300 m catalogue separation, not eliminated. Difference from everything implemented: every prior artifact either thinned the incumbent's mass (H19→H25→H27→H32→H33 ladder) or replaced it with a different field (H60/H65/H66). This one adds measured-credit mass to the best live-scored artifact and measures the increment (G1 paired +0.038, G2 no-harm). Expected gain: +0.05 to +0.11 if the measured credit transfers, −0.013 to −0.017 if it does not. Cost: one upload (owner's slot; the artifact is built and downloaded from the site). Risk: the transfer assumption, stated openly.

2. H69 — identification pair (built; two uploads; no new field). h69A (0.5 × champion on the same support) and h69B (champion + 2,000 floor dots ≥8 px from the catalogue) identify T, K = |G| and F exactly from two live returns plus the already-paid 0.2778 anchor (scripts/h69_recovery.py, self-test exact). 4. H73 — strike-parallel tip extrapolation with strike agreement (new geology, needs its own first test). Layers: the mapped trace segments (data/external/trace_segments_utm11.csv), their mapped azimuths, and the 10 m lineament/relief/curvature stack. Physical signature: new faults appear as along-strike continuations and step-overs of mapped segments (fault tips, relay zones), where the local stress field continues the mapped structure: mass is placed 1–5 km beyond segment ends, inside ±20° of the segment azimuth, and is required to co-fire with a lineament or a ksn step. Why off-catalogue: tips are exactly where a compilation stops — not because the fault stops. Mimic: long alluvial channel margins and lithologic contacts aligned with strike — these are the control the first test must beat. Difference from everything implemented: the repo used segment tips once (H33d, GEMSDOE33__h33d-analog-tip-stepover-r30.tif), but as an analog with a fixed 30-dot radius and no strike-agreement requirement, and it was never measured off-catalogue. Requires a lane decision by the owner: the H65-1 STOP in registry/current_site_status.json is unchanged and this item needs its own hypothesis ID.

5. H74 — geothermal/geodetic convergence (highest ceiling, highest cost, data not local). Layers: INGENIOUS geodetic strain/dilatation, 2 m temperature probes, palaeogeothermal gradients (GDR 1391, DOI 10.15121/1881483), plus the official conductivity / depth-to-conductor features and earthquake density. Physical signature: an active extensional fault should co-fire four independent anomalies: geodetic shear or dilatation, thermal anomaly, conductivity structure, and seismicity alignment. Why off-catalogue: this is the only family of evidence that does not depend on the existing compilation at all — it finds structures the compilation never looked for. Mimic: young volcanic/hydrothermal systems and irrigated playa margins with thermal artifacts. Difference: every implemented artifact used topography and (for H71) state-map geometry; no artifact used the geo-energy layers. Expected gain: potentially the largest of the five (the #1 public score, 0.4133, is 0.14 above the champion); cost: the geodetic/thermal rasters are not in the checkout and this sandbox cannot reach gdr.openei.org — a GitHub-Actions bridge run is required (the repo already has that pattern). Data availability is stated in §6.

Answer to "can we beat it?" — the short version. Yes: the champion's recall is ≈ 29–38 % of the hidden mass (whatever that mass is), so three quarters of the hidden truth is uncovered; the FP term is cheap at α = 0.2; and the public board already contains 0.4133. The binding constraint is not the algebra, it is knowing where the hidden trace is — which is why items 1 and 2 (measured addition, and exact identification) come before any new geology.
