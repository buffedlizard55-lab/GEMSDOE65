Method

A fault map is built from a catalogue that was assembled by people looking at evidence sufficient to convince a reviewer. That catalogue is therefore biased, and the bias is spatially structured: catalogued regions are mapped at greater density than the rest. A model that ignores this learns where mappers looked. This lane models the bias explicitly.

1. The label model (SAR, not SCAR)

With y the true fault indicator and s the catalogued indicator, the propensity is

e(x) = P(s = 1 | y = 1, x)

using only a small, predeclared attribute subset (local relief, slope, gravity-derived basin-fill proxy, lidar coverage, distance to range front) rather than all 19 classifier bands. That restriction is required by the method, not a convenience: the propensity model must be identifiable from fewer attributes than the classifier fits (Bekker & Davis, PMLR v94, Algorithm 1 and its surrounding text). A constant propensity is the SCAR model; it is implemented here as a third arm precisely because SCAR is the null hypothesis this lane exists to reject.

2. Estimation by EM

Initialisation: a balanced classifier that treats unlabelled cells as negatives, with the propensity model initialised from the positive probabilities it produces. E-step: posterior positive probability for each cell. M-step: fit the classifier on the propensity-weighted empirical risk, and refit the propensity model on the catalogued cells only. The loop stops on both stopping conditions — the log-likelihood and the propensity field must each settle (tol = 1e-3) — because the M-step here is the weighted ERM of Bekker, Robberechts & Davis, not the exact posterior maximiser, so a strictly-increasing log-likelihood test never terminates (it oscillates about 0.3%). That reversal is recorded in the repo history as a bug that was fixed rather than as a tuning choice.

The scale of e is not identifiable from labels alone, so the run is repeated for kappa in {0.5, 1, 2} and the reported arm uses the predeclared default kappa = 1. Weights are clipped at 10 as specified.

3. Risk and baseline

The classifier minimises the propensity-weighted empirical risk; the baseline minimises nnPU (non-negative PU) risk on identical folds — same draw, same design matrix, same emission rule, same budget grid. Nothing but the risk function changes between the two, which is what makes the comparison evidence rather than anecdote.

4. The holdout instrument

Hide-and-recover: whole catalogued components are withheld with a 3 px collar, all catalogue-derived features are recomputed from the visible faults only, visible faults are masked pixel-exactly, and the score is the pooled distance-weighted Tversky index (alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, R = 3 px triangular kernel) computed with the frozen template evaluator. This run: seed 2026, hide fraction 0.2, 11360 withheld positives, visible catalogue 48534 px.

Why these numbers are relative. The instrument scores the withheld pixels only. The organizer scores against every fault, including the ones already in the catalogue. The two are different denominators, so a holdout DTI of 0.068 and a leaderboard score of 0.28 are not in the same units — the holdout is a ranking device, and it is used only that way.

5. The gate, stated before the number was read

promote if and only if the SAR-propensity arm's pooled HOLDOUT-DTI is strictly greater than nnPU's and its DTI inside the highest-propensity tercile is not lower. One comparison, one draw, no selection over arms after the fact. Verdict on this run: PROMOTE (overall delta 0.005378, tercile delta 0.011799).

6. Emission rule, and why it is not a free choice

The metric is TP_w = sum over truth pixels of max over predictions of p * k(distance). Two dots standing on the same truth pixel therefore earn the credit of one. A first attempt that simply drained the highest-scoring cells in order produced 19,688 dots of which 1,195 sat within 300 m of withheld truth — a 92x enrichment — and still scored 0.000000, because those dots covered very few distinct truth pixels. Non-maximum suppression at the kernel radius fixes exactly that failure, and it is the rule this file uses: scan candidates in descending expected credit, accept only if no accepted dot lies within 3 px.

The budget is not hand-tuned. A predeclared grid of budgets {500 … 160000} is scored on the holdout, and the emission self-truncates when no candidate survives suppression. See Evidence for the curve and for the two hypotheses this lane tested and rejected.
