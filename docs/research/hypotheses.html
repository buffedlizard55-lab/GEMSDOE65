Five new geological hypotheses for the DOE GEMS Prize (competition 306)

Date 2026-10-11 · lane GEMSDOE65 (selection-bias-aware PU learning under SAR) · author: autonomous agent run, no human input · budget: 3 experiments / 2 h.

Every hypothesis below states the layer (what physical field is used), the signature/transform (how it becomes a score), why it catches faults that the USGS QFaults / INGENIOUS catalogue does not contain, how it differs from what the sibling repositories already did, the free official source, and whether that source is obtainable from this sandbox. They are ranked by expected DTI gain per unit of implementation cost.

The decisive measurement behind all of them is the two-inventory differential (evidence/mapping_bias_two_inventory.json, MEASURED, this repo): comparing the competition catalogue against the mirrored state-geologic-map fault inventory (sgmc_faults_100m.tif, 59,012 pixels more than 300 m from any catalogue pixel) gives, as AUCs of a single covariate separating "in the catalogue" from "mapped by the state but absent from the catalogue":

depth_to_base_surf 0.6935 0.3290 —
iso_grav_anom_slope 0.6101 0.4211 —

Read it as: the catalogue over-represents deep-basin-fill, low-relief, low-slope ground far from the range front; the state geologic maps over-represent exposed, high-relief, steep ground near the range front. Both are fault maps of the same terrain. That single differential is the observable form of selection bias, and it is what a SAR (selected-at-random given covariates) model is built to exploit. Note it is the inverse of the naive expectation ("steep scarps are easy to see, so they must be over-mapped") — the catalogue is a geophysics-informed compilation carrying many Inferred basin-margin faults, while state geologic maps are bedrock-exposure maps.

H65-1 — Two-inventory propensity (implemented; top candidate) — rank 1

  • Layer: the five pinned covariates (local relief, slope, gravity-derived basin-fill proxy, LiDAR coverage, distance to the range front) plus a second public fault inventory used as the reference population.
  • Signature/transform: fit q(x) = P(in catalogue | in catalogue ∪ state-map-only, x) by logistic regression on the two inventories' pixels, then invert q = e/(e + c(1-e)) to e(x; c) = c·q(x) / (1 − q(x) + c·q(x)). c is the completeness of the reference inventory — the single documented sensitivity parameter. Rank the field by P(y=1|x)·(1 − e(x)) and train with the propensity-aware empirical risk of Bekker, Robberechts & Davis (arXiv:1809.03207), weights w = min(1/e, 10), v = min((1−e)/e, 10), normaliser ρ = P(s=1) from population counts.
  • Why it catches catalogue-missing faults: 1 − e(x) is large exactly where the catalogue under-samples relative to an independent inventory of the same physical objects — measured here as exposed, high-relief, steep, near-range-front ground (bias AUCs 0.20–0.34). An expert panel working from 1 m LiDAR finds scarps there; a geophysics-inferred compilation does not. The family's own ladder says the prize is in re-ranking the same mass, not in adding mass (60GEMSDOE/docs/research/why-02778-and-what-beats-it-2026-10-11.md, OWNER-REPORT: mean credit 0.135/dot = 2.4× break-even at DTI 0.2778, so raising credit/dot to 0.150 ≈ 0.30 and to 0.19 ≈ 0.36).
  • Differs from the repo family: no sibling lane used a second inventory as a propensity reference. 60GEMSDOE used distance-to-catalogue, trace morphology, multiline corroboration and ring-peel geometry; 61GEMSDOE used LiDAR scarp descriptors. Those are all positive-evidence layers. This one is a selection-model layer: it changes the risk functional and the ranking, not the features.
  • Free official source: state geologic map fault traces via the mirrored sgmc_faults_100m.tif already in this sandbox (public-repository derivative, hash-pinned); QFaults/INGENIOUS via https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/faults and https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391 (INGENIOUS, DOI 10.15121/1881483).
  • Obtainable here: yes — both inventories are local and hash-pinned. Verified in this run: rasterising trace_segments_utm11.csv (1,126 QFaults/INGENIOUS segment rows, all 84,331 rasterised) covers 96.54 % of the catalogue at median distance 0 px (max 1 px), so the catalogue is the QFaults/INGENIOUS compilation (evidence/catalogue_identity.json).
  • Cost: low (one logistic fit per fold). Expected gain: the whole lane.

H65-2 — LiDAR-coverage-conditional detectability of the final label set — rank 2

  • Layer: lidar_cover from the official 19-band stack (12-band quantised LiDAR scarp descriptors; 0 = no LiDAR). Measured: LiDAR is valid over 3,894,460 px = 31.72 % of the grid = 75.37 % of the scored footprint.
  • Signature/transform: treat LiDAR presence as a detectability multiplier for the panel that produces the private expert truth, not as a fault predictor (measured AUC vs catalogue 0.5205 ≈ random, so it carries almost no direct fault signal). Multiply the emitted field by a monotone function of coverage and spend budget preferentially in covered ground; in uncovered ground emit only where two independent lines of evidence agree.
  • Why it catches missing faults: the final-round label set is an expert panel's map. In the 24.6 % of the footprint with no 1 m LiDAR the panel must fall back on aerial imagery and geophysics, which is precisely where both public inventories disagree with each other most (state-map-only pixels are slightly more likely in uncovered ground: AUC 0.4419 < 0.5). Disagreement between inventories is the highest-yield place to put a dot.
  • Differs from the repo family: 61GEMSDOE used LiDAR scarp descriptors as positive evidence and documented 10 failed tiles of 716 and a 1–2 m datum offset. This hypothesis uses coverage as an uncertainty variable in the emission rule.
  • Free official source: USGS 3DEP https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program, staged products at s3://prd-tnm/StagedProducts/Elevation/1m/ (the family's OCR of the official DEM-links PDF matched 722 tile/project pairs, dominated by NV_WestCentral_EarthMRI_2020_D20 (556), NV_EastCentral_2021_D21 (115), CA_SierraNevada_B22 (22)).
  • Obtainable here: the quantised layer is local; the raw 1 m tiles are not reachable from this sandbox (usgs.gov and the Dropbox mirrors return HTTP 000). Reported as a limitation, not assumed away.
  • Cost: low. Expected gain: medium; it mostly re-allocates budget.

H65-3 — Range-front structural inheritance, done properly — rank 3

  • Layer: a real range-front / basin-margin trace, not a proxy. This run compared four operationalizations (evidence/range_front_variants.json): A = 50 m isoline of det_elev above a 5 km local minimum (160,275 px, bias AUC 0.6609), B = toe-of-front (87,865 px, 0.5962), C = 30–250 m + slope ≥ 0.08 (231,990 px, 0.6178), D = basin-edge (64,060 px, 0.6202). Variant A was kept because it carries the two-inventory differential best, and it is documented as a proxy of limited geomorphic fidelity.
  • Signature/transform: distance-to-front as a covariate inside the propensity (already done) plus, in the upgrade, a front-parallel anisotropy term: emit along strike, not across it, using gems57.network.segments.
  • Why it catches missing faults: basin-margin normal faults are the dominant Quaternary structure in this extensional province; the catalogue's distance distribution to the proxy front is shifted farther than the state maps' (stored dist_range_front AUC vs state-map-only 0.6440 vs bias AUC 0.3391), i.e. the catalogue systematically omits the front-proximal segment population.
  • Differs from the repo family: 60GEMSDOE H70/H71 worked on mapped-but-uncatalogued structures; nothing in the family built a range-front surface.
  • Free official source: 10 m/1 m DEM (3DEP, above) + GSRM v2.1 strain rate (already a band in the official stack) + GeoDAWN ScienceBase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7.
  • Obtainable here: partially — DEM-derived bands are in the stack; raw 3DEP is not.
  • Cost: medium. Expected gain: medium.

H65-4 — En-echelon stepover / relay-ramp gaps — rank 4

  • Layer: catalogue trace geometry itself (segment endpoints, stepovers, hard-linked vs soft-linked tips) via gems57.network.segments.
  • Signature/transform: score the gap between two mapped segment tips whose azimuths are within ~20° and whose separation is 300–1500 m; emit one dot per relay ramp.
  • Why it catches missing faults: Quaternary normal-fault systems in this province grow by linkage; a relay ramp between two mapped segments is a real, mappable fault that a compilation drawn from older 1:250,000 sheets routinely leaves as two disjoint traces. The catalogue is 1-px-wide thin lines (3×3 erosion leaves 24 of 60,988 px), 3,199 components, median 12 px, max 360 px — i.e. it is fragmented, which is exactly the precondition for this hypothesis.
  • Differs from the repo family: h19-4-multiline-corroborated counted corroborating lines; this scores unmapped linkage geometry between them.
  • Free official source: QFaults (above) + the family's rasterised trace_segments_utm11.csv (local, verified).
  • Cost: medium. Expected gain: medium-high but narrow (few thousand pixels of candidate space), so it cannot carry a full 38,000-dot budget alone.

H65-5 — Isostatic-residual gradient lineaments (buried range fronts) — rank 5

  • Layer: iso_grav_anom_slope (isostatic residual gravity gradient, from mrdata.usgs.gov/gravity/isostatic/).
  • Signature/transform: ridge/lineament extraction on the gradient magnitude, restricted to basin fill (grav_basin_fill high, depth_to_base_surf high).
  • Why it catches missing faults: a buried range front has no surface expression but a sharp basement-depth discontinuity. This is the complement of H65-1: the catalogue over-represents exactly this environment (bias AUC 0.6935 for depth_to_base_surf, 0.6101 for the gradient), so a panel with LiDAR will not confirm many of them, while a panel with gravity will. High variance, and it fights the H65-1 ranking, so it is ranked last despite being the strongest single bias carrier.
  • Free official source: USGS isostatic gravity https://mrdata.usgs.gov/gravity/isostatic/, DOI 10.5066/P9MQRCBY / 10.5066/P9TWT2LU.
  • Obtainable here: as bands in the official stack; mrdata.usgs.gov unreachable from the sandbox.
  • Cost: medium. Expected gain: low-medium, direction uncertain.

What was validated before any submission slot was touched

The top candidate was validated on two spatially-blocked instruments built with the shared, hash-pinned evaluator (gems57.evaluate_holdout, version gems57-pooled-hide-v2, official α=0.2, β=0.8, R=300 m):

  • I1 hide-and-recover — gems57.holdout.buffered_component_draw(seed=20, hide_frac=0.20, buffer_px=3): whole 8-connected catalogue components withheld, 3 px feature-context collar removed, nearest visible catalogue pixel 3.162 px from withheld truth, 11,321 withheld positive pixels over 4 folds (NW 1,923 / NE 2,367 / SW 3,781 / SE 3,250).
  • I2 state-map-only — truth = 59,012 mapped fault pixels more than 3 px from the catalogue, scored leave-one-quadrant-out so the propensity anchor never sees the pixels it is scored on.

Leakage canary (evidence/leakage_canary.json, MEASURED): no single covariate reaches AUC 0.90 against withheld truth. Worst = dist_to_visible_catalogue 0.7548 (the legitimate control signal), then relief_local 0.5665, slope 0.5636, lidar_cover 0.5329, dist_range_front 0.5064, grav_basin_fill 0.4593.

No submission slot was spent by this session; promotion is a separate selector step, per the standing instruction.
